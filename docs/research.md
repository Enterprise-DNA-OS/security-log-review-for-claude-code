# Target research

Checked 16 November 2026. Family: security-log-review. The registry has access-governance, incident-review, threat-intelligence-operations and application-error-triage, but none is the source-policy and imported security-log review desk built here.

US$13,995 a year is ManageEngine's published annual licence for 250 log sources. It is a published configuration, not a verified customer invoice and not a claim about every 10 to 50 person business. [Vendor pricing](https://www.manageengine.com/products/eventlog/pricing-details.html). Cloud accounts and endpoints have separate price rows. The initial fit is an IT team with enough managed sources to face a five-figure bill. No named local buyer or invoice was established in this run, so buyer fit is scored conservatively.

| Criterion | Score out of 5 | Evidence and limit |
|---|---:|---|
| Annual bill | 2 | Published five-figure configuration, below the 20k anchor |
| Data-shaped | 4 | Event evidence, policies, investigations and review documents; collection stays separately scoped |
| Our buyers pay for it | 2 | IT operations fit; no verified customer invoice |
| Search demand | 3 | Qualitative alternative and pricing results; no measured volume |
| One-session buildable | 4 | Scoped review desk with batch import, not a complete SIEM |
| Import path | 4 | Documented selectable CSV reports; mapping and export reconciliation required |

Total 19/30. Compared candidates: Netwrix Auditor 1/4/2/3/4/4 = 18, held because its public pricing did not establish an annual amount; Docparser 1/4/2/3/4/5 = 19, rejected before ranking because its public Business plan is below five figures annually; Front 4/3/3/4/2/3 = 19 for an illustrative 50-seat Enterprise configuration, rejected for dependence on a live shared inbox and overlap with our mail offering. Filter first, then rank eligible targets. No extra target was queued.

The [vendor search documentation](https://www.manageengine.com/products/eventlog/help/StandaloneManagedServer-UserGuide/SearchLogs/how-to-search.html) describes saved searches and exports. The [report export guide](https://www.manageengine.com/products/eventlog/help/StandaloneManagedServer-UserGuide/EventLogAnalyzerReports/report-export-and-customization.html) documents CSV and selectable columns. The [partial export note](https://pitstop.manageengine.com/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-troubleshoot-incomplete-or-partial-data-exports-in-eventlog-analyzer) makes count reconciliation necessary. Default aliases and example rows are implementation fixtures, not a claim that a real vendor export was tested.

The page compares the owned review workflow and states what needs separate delivery. It does not claim parity with collectors, automated threat detection, enterprise security controls or the incumbent's entire product. Included analysis questions are demonstrable; no claim is made that the incumbent cannot be customised to answer them.

Other candidate sources: [Netwrix pricing](https://netwrix.com/en/pricing/), [Docparser pricing](https://docparser.com/pricing), [Front pricing](https://front.com/pricing). A qualitative user complaint about support and reporting is not used as an established product defect.
